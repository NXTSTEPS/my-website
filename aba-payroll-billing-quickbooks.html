ABA Payroll and Billing into QuickBooks, Without Re-Keying

Every pay period, the same claims get typed into QuickBooks again. It doesn't have to work that way.

By Jerry Goldberg, NxtSteps — an accountant who builds back-office automation and works with each client directly.

Every pay period, someone takes the claims export, the provider pay rates and the HR pay schedule, and re-keys payroll and claims into QuickBooks as vendor bills and payer invoices. If the practice runs more than one entity, that happens once for every QuickBooks company. It's slow, it's easy to get wrong, and it usually lands on one bookkeeper.

This page is for owners and bookkeepers at ABA and behavioral health practices who want payroll and billing to land in QuickBooks correctly the first time, without adding a new platform to learn.

Where ABA payroll and billing leak time

  • Data in several places: the claims export, the provider pay-rate tab, the HR pay schedule and the client roster aren't connected, so someone stitches them together by hand.
  • The same work, once per entity: each agency or entity has its own QuickBooks company, so every step is repeated for each one.
  • Rules that are easy to get wrong: a bill goes to the staffing agency when there is one and to the rendering BCBA when there isn't, and each line needs the right item (BCBA or BT), the CPT code and the units as quantity.
  • Two pay schedules in one run: providers paid twice a month and providers paid monthly sit in the same export, and the wrong ones get pulled into a run.
  • Duplicate vendors: a name spelled one way in the spreadsheet and another way in QuickBooks creates a second vendor, and reporting breaks.
  • No closing check: there's no quick way to see bills without invoices, or invoices without bills, before the period closes.

The cost isn't only the typing. It's the error hunt afterwards: a duplicate vendor, a bill with no matching invoice, a provider pulled into the wrong run, found at month-end instead of on the day.

What the ABA Back-Office Autopilot does

It turns each pay period's claims export into provider bills and payer invoices in QuickBooks, for each entity, in three steps the bookkeeper runs from one control sheet.

1. Check. Pick the month, the company (or all of them) and the pay frequency. Provider names and items are checked against QuickBooks before anything is created, only for the people in this period's claims export. Each mismatch gets a suggested match and a simple choice: use the match, create new, or skip. No duplicate vendors.

2. Approve and create. The bookkeeper approves each line, then the system creates the vendor bills and payer invoices in the right QuickBooks company. Bills go to the staffing agency or the rendering provider, with the CPT code and the period-end date on them.

3. Close out. A reconciliation counts bills without invoices and invoices without bills, broken down by vendor and by customer, so the period closes clean instead of being found out at month-end.

  • It works from the claims export and the QuickBooks you already use. No new practice management or billing software.
  • A pay-frequency filter keeps the twice-monthly run from touching monthly providers.
  • Your existing workbooks stay as they are. Nothing is overwritten.
  • It runs in your own accounts, with an install guide, a monthly user guide and a one-page pay run checklist written for the bookkeeper who runs it.
